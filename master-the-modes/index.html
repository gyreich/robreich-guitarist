The approach

Why the modes usually sound alike, and the fix

Most guitarists learn the modes as shapes on the fretboard, each one starting on a different note of the same major scale. Played that way, they all sound alike, because they're all the same notes.

Master the Modes takes the opposite approach. Every mode starts on C, so the difference you hear is the mode itself. Once you can hear what each interval does, you can find that color in any key and use it on purpose, in real music.

  • Hear the difference between modes before you memorize anything
  • Learn the interval formula that unlocks each mode in any key
  • See how one note, the 3rd, turns major into minor
  • Connect the modes to scales you already know, like the minor pentatonic
  • Practice with real grooves, not a metronome

Is Master the Modes really free?

Yes. Enter your email address and you get the whole course: the one-hour Master Class, the illustrated guide and all 14 play-along tracks. There is nothing to buy.

Why are all seven modes in the key of C?

Playing every mode from the same note means the only thing that changes is the mode itself, so you can hear exactly what makes each one sound the way it does.

What level of guitarist is the course for?

Players who know a few scales and want to understand and use the modes musically. If you can play the major scale or the minor pentatonic, you're ready.
